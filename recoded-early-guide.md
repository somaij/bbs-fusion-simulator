<h1>KH Re:coded Early Game Guide – Command Synthesis Priorities</h1>
<p>Your first synthesis priority in <strong>Kingdom Hearts Re:coded</strong> is simple: get <strong>Fira</strong> as soon as you arrive in Traverse Town. Destiny Islands hands you a Fire Edge at the end of the world, and combining it with Fire the moment you land in Traverse Town gives you a meaningful magic upgrade before the first real fights begin. Everything else in the early game builds on that foundation.</p>

<p>If you have not started yet, giving up the Sword is widely recommended. Both Rod and Shield paths that drop the Sword give you Cure or Fire immediately, which are far more useful early than the physical options the Sword provides.</p>

<h2>🏝️ Destiny Islands</h2>
<p>Destiny Islands is a short tutorial world. Your starting commands depend on the weapon choices you make at the beginning of the game, so synthesis may or may not be possible here. The important thing is the reward at the end: a <strong>Fire Edge</strong>. Save it for Traverse Town.</p>

<p>By the end of Olympus Coliseum you should have Fira, Blizzara, and Thundara for magic; elemental Edge commands for physical-magic hybrids; Cura for healing; and the Raid variants for ranged coverage. That deck carries you comfortably through the next several worlds.</p>

<h2>Why You Should Level Commands Before Synthesizing</h2>
<p>Every command earns <strong>Command Points (CP)</strong> passively just by being installed in your deck when you defeat enemies. You do not need to actively use it. As CP accumulates the command levels up, becoming stronger and dealing more damage up to a maximum of level 100.</p>
<p>When you synthesize two commands, the result inherits a level head-start based on how leveled the ingredients were. Two level 1 ingredients produce a level 1 result. Higher-level ingredients can produce a result that starts noticeably higher, reducing the grind on the new command.</p>
<p>There is also a chance of earning an <strong>Overspec</strong> when using leveled ingredients: a special bonus on the result such as a faster gauge fill rate, reduced reload time, or bonus starting levels. Overspecs are more likely with higher-level ingredients.</p>
<p>The practical takeaway is to let commands sit in your deck for a world or two before committing them to synthesis, especially before expensive endgame recipes like Firaga, Curaga, or Aeroga.</p>

<h2>Where to Go Next</h2>
<p>Once you have the above covered, the next upgrade tier is the -ga spells: <strong>Firaga</strong> (Fira + Heat Storm), <strong>Blizzaga</strong> (Ice Dive + Blizzara), and <strong>Thundaga</strong> (Thundara + Spark Storm). Getting <strong>Curaga</strong> (Heat Dive + Cura) should also be a priority once Heat Dive is within reach. Every command you synthesize now is a potential ingredient for something stronger later.</p>

<h2>Tips</h2>
<ul>
    <li><strong>Never discard Cure.</strong> It is an ingredient in Rising Strike, Fira, Blizzara, Thundara, and Cura. Keep spares whenever possible.</li>
    <li>Use cross-element routes (Blizzard + Fire, Thunder + Fire) to upgrade magic without spending Cure when you need it elsewhere.</li>
    <li>Strike Raid is worth picking up purely to unlock the Raid variants. You do not need to equip it after synthesizing.</li>
    <li>Slow is a weak battle command but an excellent synthesis ingredient. Convert it to Cura as soon as you can.</li>
</ul>
